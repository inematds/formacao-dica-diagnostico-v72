A small business is not a small company: the DICA method

A nine-person clinic is not a miniature big company. The owner decides alone, there's no IT, WhatsApp is the front door to everything, and a lot of the rules live in her head. That's why the diagnosis comes before any tool, and it's where the DICA method starts.

02 · The portrait of the small business

Four traits that change how you diagnose.

1. The owner decides alone

There is no board and no purchasing department. The person who answers the interview is the one who approves, pays, and uses it.

That speeds up the decision, but everything depends on one person's schedule and mood.

At Clínica Vila Serena (fictional)The owner sees patients in the morning and handles administration in the afternoon. The conversation with you fits between the two.

2. There is no IT

The system password, the WhatsApp account, and the spreadsheet stay with whoever set them up, sometimes a nephew.

Before you think about integrating, find out who has access to what.

3. WhatsApp is the front door

Scheduling, questions, complaints, and payments all arrive on the same phone.

That's why the WhatsApp rules (lesson 7) belong in the diagnosis, not only in the build.

4. The rules live in their head

The owner knows how to do it but not how to describe it. Prices, exceptions, and "the way we do things here" were never written down.

Lessons 4 and 5 teach you how to draw this out without asking the owner to come up with rules alone.

03 · Template: the 5-line sheet

Fill it in before the interview with what you already know; the rest becomes questions. Replace what is between < > with your own.

ficha-5-linhas.md
# 5-line sheet — <business name> (<city>, <date>)

1. Who decides: <name and role; who else needs to agree>
2. Main channel: <how the customer arrives and talks: WhatsApp, phone, counter>
3. Who answers today: <person, hours, what happens outside hours>
4. What can't stop: <the activity that, if it stops for a day, costs money>
5. Where the information lives: <system, spreadsheet, notebook, someone's head>

Don't know yet: <what is missing and which interview question will answer it>
Filled-in example (fictional)
# 5-line sheet — Clínica Vila Serena (fictional)

1. Who decides: the owner, alone; the partner weighs in on spending
2. Main channel: the front desk WhatsApp (one phone)
3. Who answers today: the receptionist, 8 a.m. to 6 p.m.; at night, the owner on her own phone
4. What can't stop: the physical therapists' schedule
5. Where the information lives: schedule in the clinic's system; prices and insurance plans in the team's heads

Don't know yet: how many messages arrive per day (interview question 5)
Simulation: illustrative example
  • Each line has a concrete answer (a name, a channel, a place), not an adjective.
  • There is at least one "don't know yet" line, tied to an interview question.
  • No line mentions an AI tool.
  • Writing the solution on the sheet ("needs a robot").
  • Leaving line 1 vague: "the company decides".
  • Making up what you don't know instead of marking "don't know yet".

5 min

Done when: both sentences exist, and the second one names at least one thing that is left for after the diagnosis.
