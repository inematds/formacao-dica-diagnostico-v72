Lesson 3 · 18 min

The 5 areas: customer service, sales, marketing, management, finance

Without scoring, your suggestion sounds like an opinion. With a score, it becomes a diagnosis the owner can discuss. You go through the 5 areas with 3 questions each, score pain, urgency, and readiness from 0 to 3, and write next to each score why you gave it.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  2. 2The symptoms the owner has already gotten used to

Goal: score the business's 5 areas (pain, urgency, readiness) with a written criterion for each score and pick where to look first.

02 · The 0–3 matrix, with written criteria

Three scores per area. The criterion comes before the score.

1. Pain

0 = the owner sees no problem. 1 = complains in general, no specific case. 2 = tells a recent case. 3 = tells a recent case and says what it costs (time, lost customer) or has already tried to fix it.

The score comes from what they said in the interview, not from your impression.

2. Urgency

0 = can wait more than six months. 1 = this quarter. 2 = this month. 3 = it's getting in the way right now and they asked for a deadline.

Urgency without pain is anxiety; pain without urgency gets noted for a later phase.

3. Readiness

0 = nothing written down, nobody knows. 1 = it's in the head of someone who is available. 2 = partly in a spreadsheet or system. 3 = organized in one place, with someone who looks after it.

Low readiness doesn't rule out the area: it means the first step is to organize the information.

4. Add up and talk it through

Add up the three scores for each area and show the table to the owner in the feedback session. They agree or correct each score.

When the owner corrects a score with a case, the criterion gets stronger. When they correct it without a case, note it and keep your criterion.

Comparison (fictional)At Imobiliária Ponto Firme (4 agents), sales scored higher than customer service: the proposal goes out the day after the visit. At Vila Serena, customer service leads because of the evening messages.
