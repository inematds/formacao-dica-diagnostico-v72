Lesson 6 · 18 min

The opportunity map: 6 axes, 4 quadrants, and what not to recommend

Many ideas come out of the interview, and the new consultant picks the most interesting one. The diagnosis picks the one that gives a fast result safely, so the trust pays for the next ones. You score each candidate on 6 axes, place it on the 4-quadrant chart, and also write down what you won't recommend, and why.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1The question before the axes: does it need AI?
  2. 2The 6 axes from 0 to 3 and how to work out the score
  3. 3The 4 quadrants and the first project
  4. 4How to say what stays out

Goal: score 5 candidates on the 6 axes, recommend 1, point out 1 that doesn't need AI, and write the reason for each decision.

01 · The 4 quadrants

Horizontal axis: ease. Vertical axis: impact. Click each quadrant.

Click each block in the diagram to see what it does.

The chart answers "why this one before that one?" better than any argument.

02 · From the catalog to the recommendation

Four steps, in this order.

1. Does it need AI?

For each candidate, ask first: can you stop doing it altogether? Would a fixed rule solve it (a reminder, a spreadsheet, an automatic message from the system itself)?

If a fixed rule solves it, it is cheaper and more predictable. The full ladder is in prof2030-consultor, lesson 3.

At Vila Serena (fictional)An appointment reminder the day before is a fixed rule: the scheduling system sends it. It doesn't need AI.

2. The 6 axes

Impact (hours per week or lost clients, counted by the owner), ease (data ready, a tool that already exists), urgency, the owner's willingness, risk, and dependence on human judgment.

Risk and human dependence count against: a high score on them makes automation harder and more dangerous. The criteria for each score are in the template below.

3. The math

Score = (impact + ease + urgency + willingness) − (risk + human dependence).

The math doesn't decide on its own: it organizes the conversation. If the highest score goes against common sense, revise the criteria, not the sum.

4. Recommend and cut

One recommendation, not five. And one or two that stay out, each with the reason in one line.

Tell "not now" (comes back after the first result) apart from "doesn't need AI" (a fixed rule solves it).

Comparison (fictional)Imobiliária Ponto Firme: proposal with 3 similar properties right after the visit (first); listing descriptions (bonus); property price forecast (not now: no organized data).

06 · Summary

Does it need AI?Before scoring: don't do it, use a fixed rule, or use AI. A fixed rule solves a lot.
6 axes, one calculationImpact, ease, urgency and willingness add up; risk and human dependency subtract.
One yes, a few nosOne recommendation from the "first" quadrant, and the cuts with a reason and a note on when they come back.
