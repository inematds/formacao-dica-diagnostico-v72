Lesson 2 · 20 min

The interview with the owner: prepare in 20 minutes, the 15 questions, the silence

The diagnosis has already been agreed on; now comes the conversation that decides whether it will be a good one. You show up prepared, ask to record and say what it's for, ask 15 questions always in the same order, and let the owner talk. The real pain shows up in a case from last week, almost never in the first answer.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1Prepare for the conversation in 20 minutes
  2. 2Ask to record, stating the purpose, the tool, and the deadline
  3. 3Ask the 15 questions in 5 blocks and use silence
  4. 4Practice in a mock interview with the clinic owner

Goal: run the diagnosis interview with the 15 questions, ask for recording with clear consent, and tell real pain from false pain.

02 · Question blocks and the pain test

Three questions per block, in 5 blocks. The order matters more than the wording.

1. Context and flow

Start with what the owner likes to talk about: what the business does, how it started, who is on the team.

Then the customer's path, from first contact to payment, and what stalls when the owner isn't there.

2. Bottleneck

Only once the owner is at ease: the most tedious task of the week, where customers get lost, what gets redone every week.

This is where the candidates for an assistant show up. Write them down, don't propose anything.

3. Dream and fear

Dream before fear: what needs to be different in 12 months, what they would do with free time.

Last: what has already gone wrong with technology, what worries them about AI, and who else needs to agree.

4. Real or false pain

Real pain has a recent case, a cost (in time, lost customers, or money), and some urgency. False pain sounds like a speech: "I'd like to improve everything a little."

When in doubt, ask three questions: "give me an example from the last time," "how much does that cost you in time or customers," "if nothing changes, where will you be in 6 months."

When they don't know the numberDon't guess. Agree on a simple count for one week (a sheet of paper at the front desk, a tally mark for each missed message) and use that number in the report. It becomes the baseline: the starting point for comparing later.

# Diagnostic interview — <business> — <date>
# Preparation (20 min): 3 pain hypotheses + 2 specific questions
Hypotheses: 1) <...> 2) <...> 3) <...>
Specific questions: a) <...> b) <...>

## Block 1 — Context
1. Tell me in one sentence what <company> does and who it's for.
2. How long have you been in business? How did it start?
3. How many people are on the team today? What's your role day to day?

## Block 2 — Flow
4. Walk me through the path of a <client/patient> from the moment they find you until they pay.
5. Which channels do they use to reach you? How many messages come in on a normal day?
6. When you're not there, what stops?

## Block 3 — Bottleneck
7. What's the most annoying thing about your week, the one you wish you didn't have to do?
8. Where do you lose <client/patient>: before, during, or after the service?
9. Is there any task you redo every week?

## Block 4 — Dream
10. 12 months from now, what needs to be different for you to be satisfied?
11. If you gained a few free hours a week, what would you use them for?
12. What kind of <client/patient> would you like to have more of?

## Block 5 — Fear
13. Have you ever tried a technology that didn't work out? What happened?
14. What worries you when you think about AI serving your <client/patient>?
15. Who else needs to agree for us to move forward?

Simulation: illustrative example
  • The script has the 15 questions with the words of the niche (patient, buyer, client).
  • The consent states the purpose, the tool, where it's kept, and when it's deleted.
  • The closing has a date for the follow-up and a counting task, when a number is missing.

Your voice and what the owner tells you are personal data. At a clinic, the conversation can touch on a patient's health data, which LGPD (Brazil's data protection law, Law 13,709/2018, art. 5, II) calls sensitive data.

That's why you ask them not to mention names, and why there's a deadline to delete. To choose the transcription tool with data in mind, see the Data guard in the Auditor course (link at the end of the lesson).
