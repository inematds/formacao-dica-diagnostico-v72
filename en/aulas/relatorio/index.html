Lesson 8 · 20 min

The one-page report in the owner's language + 30-day plan

Nobody reads a long report. The owner wants to know what you found, what you recommend, what's left out, and what happens each week. All on one page, in their own words, no acronyms. This lesson pulls together what you did in the course and builds your plan with dates.

02 · Write in the owner's language

Four rules that apply to the whole page.

1. Result, not technology

Swap the technical noun for what changes on their Monday.

"Assistant with a knowledge base" becomes "answers just like the front desk, using your insurance table".

2. Their words

Quote the owner at least twice, using the exact sentence you noted in the interview.

They read it and recognize their own problem, said the way they say it.

3. The limit, said up front

Say what AI doesn't do: it doesn't decide for them, it doesn't get everything right at the start, it doesn't know what isn't written down, it doesn't update itself.

That's why the plan starts with human approval. Said up front, this reassures; discovered later, it becomes a complaint.

4. The grandparent test

Read it out loud to someone who isn't in the field. If the person asks "what is that?", rewrite it.

Would you say that sentence at the counter of the bakery? If not, it's jargon.

checklist-antes-de-enviar.txt
[ ] Fits on one page
[ ] No technical acronym without an explanation
[ ] At least 2 literal sentences from the owner, in quotes
[ ] Each finding has evidence (case, count, or score)
[ ] One recommendation, not several
[ ] What is left out is written down, with a reason
[ ] Each week has a milestone the owner can see
[ ] The limit of AI is stated
[ ] No number without a source; no client personal data
[ ] Passed the grandparent test
